# Employee Attrition & Workforce Analytics — Business Insights

## Executive Summary

The analysis of 1,470 employees shows an overall observed attrition rate of 16.12%.

Several workforce characteristics show meaningful differences in observed attrition rates, particularly overtime, tenure, salary level, job satisfaction, and work-life balance.

These findings identify employee groups that may warrant further HR investigation. They should be interpreted as associations within the dataset rather than evidence of causation.

---

## 1. Early-Tenure Employees

Employees with 0–1 years at the company had an observed attrition rate of 34.88%.

This compares with:

- 2–3 years: 18.43%
- 4–5 years: 13.07%
- 6–10 years: 12.28%
- 10+ years: 8.13%

### Business Interpretation

Attrition is substantially higher among employees in the earliest tenure group.

Potential areas for HR investigation include onboarding experience, early career development, role expectations, workload, and career progression.

---

## 2. Overtime

Employees working overtime had an observed attrition rate of 30.53%.

Employees without overtime had an observed attrition rate of 10.44%.

### Business Interpretation

The large difference suggests that workload and overtime patterns warrant further investigation.

HR teams could examine workload distribution, overtime frequency, staffing levels, and employee experience among overtime employees.

---

## 3. Compensation

Employees earning below 3,000 had an observed attrition rate of 28.61%.

The observed rate among employees earning 12,000+ was 5.64%.

### Business Interpretation

Lower-income employee groups show higher observed attrition in this dataset.

Further analysis could examine compensation relative to job role, job level, tenure, and career progression before making compensation-related decisions.

---

## 5. Work-Life Balance

Observed attrition by work-life balance:

|---|---:|

## 7. Job Role

Some job roles show substantially different observed attrition rates.

Examples:

- Sales Representative: 39.76%
- Laboratory Technician: 23.94%
- Human Resources: 23.08%
- Sales Executive: 17.48%
- Research Scientist: 16.10%
- Manufacturing Director: 6.90%
- Healthcare Representative: 6.87%
- Manager: 4.90%
- Research Director: 2.50%

### Business Interpretation

Job-role-level differences suggest that workforce retention analysis should consider role-specific characteristics.

Factors such as compensation, tenure, overtime, career progression, and job level can be examined alongside job role.

---

## 8. Statistical Evidence

Statistical testing identified significant associations between attrition and several variables.

### Categorical Variables

Chi-square testing identified statistically significant associations for:

- Overtime
- Job Satisfaction
- Work-Life Balance
- Department
- Business Travel
- Marital Status
- Job Level

### Numeric Variables

Mann-Whitney U testing identified statistically significant differences for:

- Monthly Income
- Years at Company
- Age
- Total Working Years
- Years Since Last Promotion

Effect sizes were also calculated to provide context around the magnitude of observed relationships.

---

## 9. Recommended Areas for Further Investigation

Based on the observed patterns, HR analysis could investigate:

### Early-Tenure Retention
- Review onboarding processes.
- Analyze employee experience during the first 1–3 years.
- Examine early career progression.

### Overtime
- Review overtime frequency and workload distribution.
- Examine staffing requirements in high-overtime teams.
- Compare overtime with satisfaction and work-life balance.

### Compensation
- Compare compensation across equivalent roles and job levels.
- Examine salary progression with tenure.
- Investigate lower-income employee groups in greater detail.

### Employee Experience
- Examine satisfaction and work-life balance patterns.
- Compare employee experience across departments and roles.

### Career Progression
- Investigate employees with long periods since their last promotion.
- Examine whether career progression differs by role or department.

---

## 10. Important Analytical Limitation

This analysis identifies patterns and statistical associations within the dataset.

It does **not** establish that overtime, salary, satisfaction, tenure, or any other variable directly causes employee attrition.

The employee segmentation created for this project is descriptive and should not be interpreted as an individual employee risk prediction model.

The dataset is fictional and therefore should not be treated as representative of any specific organization's workforce.

---

## Final Business Takeaway

The analysis indicates that employee attrition is not evenly distributed across the workforce.

The largest differences are observed across:

- Early vs. long tenure
- Overtime vs. non-overtime employees
- Lower vs. higher income groups
- Job satisfaction levels
- Work-life balance levels
- Job roles and departments

These patterns provide a starting point for HR teams to investigate workforce retention using organization-specific data and additional business context.
